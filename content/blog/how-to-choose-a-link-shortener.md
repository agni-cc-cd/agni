Choosing a link shortener looks trivial until you have a thousand live links on the wrong one. The redirect all of them do is a solved problem; the differences that will bite you later are analytics depth, slug rules, pricing that moves, and what happens to your links if the vendor changes direction. This guide is a practical checklist for how to choose a link shortener — the questions to ask in the first hour so you are not re-deciding in month six. Work through it with your real usage in mind, not the marketing page's feature grid.

## Start With the Job, Not the Tool

Before comparing anything, write down three lines: what you will shorten, how many links per month, and what you need to know afterward. A solo creator sharing five links wants something different from a marketing team running forty placements a month, and almost every pricing surprise comes from a mismatch at this step.

The jobs generally sort into four buckets:

- **Occasional sharing.** A handful of links, mostly for neatness. Almost any tool works; stability is the only real criterion.
- **Campaign attribution.** Many placements, many channels. Analytics depth and one-link-per-placement workflows dominate.
- **Offline and print.** QR codes, posters, packaging. Link longevity and the ability to repoint destinations are critical.
- **Programmatic use.** Links generated by scripts or apps. API quality and rate limits decide feasibility.

Once the job is named, the checklist below is fast.

## The Checklist

### 1. What does each click actually record?

This is the first question and the one vendors answer most vaguely. Totals are table stakes. Look for per-click dimensions: country and city, network (ASN, datacenter), protocol and TLS version, referrer, device, browser, OS, and timestamp. If the sales page says "advanced analytics" without naming fields, ask support to name them.

### 2. Can you get the raw data?

Aggregates make reports; raw click rows make answers. When a total looks strange, you want an activity feed filterable by date range — not a support ticket. If the tool only ever shows you charts, you are renting conclusions, not owning data.

### 3. What are the slug rules?

Custom back-halves turn links into readable assets (`agni.cc.cd/pricing-guide`). Check whether slugs are free or gated, whether there is a character minimum, and whether you can edit the destination later. Repointing matters enormously for print and partner links. For naming systems that scale, see [custom short links](https://agni.cc.cd/blog/custom-short-links).

### 4. What is the redirect doing to your counts?

Temporary redirects (302) keep every visit measurable; permanent redirects (301) let caches skip the short link and quietly erase clicks. The semantics are in [RFC 9110](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110) if you want the source. Also ask where the redirect resolves: edge lookups (agni resolves slugs KV-first on Cloudflare's network) keep the redirect fast wherever your audience is.

### 5. What does it cost at your real volume, in month seven?

Free tiers are where the traps live: caps on links per month, custom slugs behind a paywall, analytics truncated after 30 days, export fees. Price your actual usage — links, team members, retention — and read the pricing page for what is *excluded*, not what is listed. The [pricing comparison for agni](https://agni.cc.cd/pricing) is deliberately one page; use that format as the standard you hold others to.

### 6. Who owns the links and the data?

Links are long-lived assets. If you delete your account or the product shuts down, what happens to links printed on physical material? The safe arrangement: you can export or query your links and stats (agni has a REST API for creating, listing, and deleting links and reading stats), and the analytics belong to the link owner.

### 7. Does it fit your auth and workflow?

Teams have identity policies. Support for email, phone, Google, and GitHub sign-in covers most cases; guest creation covers the one-off. No link shortener should ever need a credit card for the free tier — treat that as a tell about where the product is heading.

## Red Flags Worth Walking Away From

A few patterns predict regret reliably:

- **Interstitial ads on redirects.** The visitor sees an ad before your page. It taxes your audience's trust and pollutes your data.
- **Anonymous links with no owner.** Links created without an account cannot be edited, measured, or reclaimed. Convenient today, irrecoverable tomorrow.
- **"Lifetime" deals from young vendors.** Link longevity requires a business that lasts. Ask how the company makes money; vague answers are answers.
- **Analytics that gate history.** If data older than 30 days disappears on your plan, your year-over-year reporting is impossible by design.
- **Silent redirect changes.** Vendors who switch redirect types or interpose pages without notice are telling you how they treat your links.

## Counting the Real Cost

The price on the pricing page is the smallest cost in a link tool. The rest are time and risk costs that never appear on an invoice.

- **Naming and triage time.** A tool without free custom slugs costs you minutes per link forever, as you decode `x7Kq2` in every report and handoff.
- **Reconstruction time.** Aggregates-only analytics turn every "why" question into a manual investigation across channels and screenshots.
- **Migration time.** The cost you are trying to avoid by reading this guide. It scales with how many links you printed and posted while on the wrong tool.
- **Risk to printed assets.** A vendor that deletes unclaimed links, injects redirect pages, or changes behavior quietly puts your physical inventory at risk — the most expensive kind of mistake because it cannot be undone remotely.

This is why the checklist starts with analytics depth and slug rules rather than price: those two drive all four costs. ## A Ten-Minute Evaluation Script

If you want to decide today, run this script against your top two candidates:

1. Create three links with custom slugs — one per mock placement.
2. Click each once from a phone and once from a desktop.
3. Open the analytics: can you see both clicks separately, with device, location, and timestamp?
4. Repoint one link's destination and confirm the short URL still works.
5. Filter the activity feed to today only.
6. Find the export or API path for your links and stats.

If any step fails or hides behind an upgrade, the tool will frustrate you at exactly the moment the campaign is live. On agni, every step above is part of the free product — no trial clock, no feature gate — and you can run the script yourself in about five minutes. It is worth doing the same script on a second candidate the same afternoon; the differences are much sharper when the exercises are fresh in memory than when they are descriptions on two marketing pages.

One extra probe worth adding if links will appear in print: create a slug, "print" it (write it on paper), then repoint the destination and confirm the written URL still resolves to the new page. That single behavior — the mapping is yours to edit — is what protects every physical asset you will ever create, and not every tool treats it as a first-class feature.

## FAQ

### How do I choose a link shortener?

Start from your usage: how many links, which channels, and what you need to learn from the clicks. Then check analytics depth (per-click dimensions plus raw data), slug rules, redirect behavior, real-volume pricing, and data ownership. Run a five-minute test: create links, click them, read the analytics, repoint one, and find the export path.

### What is the most important feature in a link shortener?

Per-click analytics you can filter. The redirect is commoditized; the ability to know which placement worked, from where, and on what device is what you will actually use every week.

### Are free link shorteners good enough?

For most individuals and small teams, yes — if the free tier includes custom slugs, full click data, and stable links. The failure mode is not price but hidden limits: link caps, truncated history, or features that vanish after a trial. Read what is excluded.

### Should I worry about my short links breaking?

Choose a tool with a business model that does not require your links to become ad inventory. Links created under your account, editable destinations, and an API for export are the practical safeguards; interstitial ads and anonymous link creation are the risk factors.

### Is it better to use a shortener with custom domains?

Custom domains add branding but usually cost money and add DNS setup. If readable, trackable links matter more than having your own domain in the URL, a free tool with custom back-halves covers the job. Decide the domain question first; everything else follows.
